                  One seed pair per line: revealed server seed, client seed, optional nonce range (e.g. 0-4999; default 10,000 rolls from the start nonce) and optional hashed server seed (Keel checks SHA-256). server=… client=… nonce=… hashed=… also works (use client= for a numeric client seed). Stake shows the server seed only after you rotate the pair; a hashed seed alone cannot be replayed. Seeds stay on this page (not saved).

                  How to read the results
                  • Lasted (life): how much of the table a card actually played. 100% means it played every bet without going broke.
                  • Drop: the worst fall from a high point during a session, as a share of the bank.
                  • Broke (ruin): the share of sessions where the bank could not cover the next bet.
                  • Green: the share of sessions that finished ahead. Luck, not an edge.
                  • Stop autoplay (v10, exactly as on Stake): conditions are checked top to bottom after every bet result, starting at bet 1, with all counters (streaks, wins, losses, games, net gain) at 0 when the session starts. A Stop autoplay whose condition is met ends the session immediately, on any bet, including bet 1; there is no minimum. Example: “On Streak lower than 2 Games, Stop autoplay” is already true after bet 1, so it stops after one bet. The card detail and Copy for Stake show “Warning: this stops on the first bet” for any such condition. Scoring: life = bets actually played / bets per test for every session (stopped or busted); ruin counts busted sessions only; green = share of sessions finishing ahead. Stopping does not beat the house: it only lowers the total wagered.
                  • Max conditions: the most condition blocks any card may have (whole number 0–35, default 20). New cards get 0 up to this many, mutation never adds past it, and crossover, saved hall-of-fame cards and the opening classics are trimmed to it (keeping the first blocks in order). 0 means every card is base settings only.
                  • Memory: Keel keeps the hall of fame (8 cards), the top 32 confirmed cards and counts of which actions and conditions show up in high-scoring cards. Every new run seeds its first table from them, and new and mutated cards lean toward those parts. Reload, Apply and relearn and New run keep this memory; only Forget memory wipes it. If the settings changed, saved cards are trimmed to Max conditions and re-confirmed under the new settings before any can become the record. Memory makes the search faster; it does not make any card beat the house.
                  • House price vs. sample: the house price is what a card is expected to give up for every dollar wagered. The sample is what these few sessions happened to show; it is noisy, so the house price is the honest number.
                  • Ranks: Closest favors cards that keep playing with few busts and small drops. Lasts favors playing the longest, nothing else. Green favors finishing ahead most often. Caliber balances lasting, busts and drops, and penalizes drifting from the house price. Custom uses your five sliders (saved): a weighted average of life and % finished ahead (counted up) and ruin, drop and house price (counted down; a house price of 10% of the bank per session is the full penalty).
                  • Chart: the yellow line is the confirmed record card; it only moves when a full confirmation beats it. The gray line is classic martingale on the same rolls.
                  • Current top strategy (gold panel): the confirmed record. A card only takes it after a full confirmation and beating the record on 60 fresh holdout sessions neither card was picked on; since v17 a winner keeps playing paired sessions against the record (up to 420) and is dropped as early luck if its gain fades below the Min gain, before the 5,000-session deep check. The gold ★ marks it in the list.
                  • Families: each card is tagged by win-chance band (<10, 10–40, 40–60, 60–90, 90–98), whether it can stop, whether it escalates the amount, and its most-used condition. Near-duplicates are penalised when breeding and no family may fill more than 12 of 64 seats, so the search keeps a spread of styles. The best card of each family is kept in memory.
                  • Search: every card gets 3 sessions, the best 16 race on 6 more, the top 8 get a full confirmation. Each generation also tries small one-setting nudges of the record, and mutation gets stronger the longer nothing beats the record.
                  • Best per goal: the confirmed card that lasts longest, has the smallest drop, finishes ahead most often, and pays the lowest house price per session. Tap one to open it.
                  • Exploration (Low / Balanced / High, saved): how hard each run looks for new strategies instead of tweaking old winners. Balanced seeds a new run with at most 8 saved cards (the record plus one per family), keeps saved cards out of breeding for the first 5 generations (the record excepted), caps learned-part bias at 25% of new conditions and fades it every run, skips record tweaks for the first 3 generations and then allows 3 per generation, holds any family to 6 seats and near-copies of the record to 4, and reserves 12 seats for novel cards. The line under the live status shows how many families are in this generation, how many families this run has found that memory had never seen, and how many seats are variants of the record. Since v16.1 the levels also differ in how far they search: Low mutation x0.7, 0.6x the random new cards, reseed after 150 stale generations; Balanced mutation x1, reseed 24 fresh cards after 100 stale generations; High mutation x2 (win chance and condition values move twice as far, conditions are added or removed more often), 1.5x the random new cards, 24 novel seats, up to 4 tries per new card to land in a niche memory has not seen, learned-part pull x0.4, and 32 fresh cards reseeded after 40 stale generations (then every 20). The line under the live status reads e.g. “Exploration High: 55% fresh/novel seats, mutation x2, reseed after 40 stale gens” for the level the current run is actually using; if you change the level mid-run it says the new level applies on New run. “New families” stays small because there are only about 125 families in total; the niche count is the better sign of exploration. Takes effect on the next run.
                  • Export / Import memory: saves everything Keel has learned to a JSON file, or merges one in (e.g. between Safari and Chrome). Imported cards are cleaned (98% win chance, Max conditions, old per-card bet caps dropped; any card or record that relied on a cap loses its results and re-confirms) and must re-confirm before one can become the record.
                  • Bank: $1 to $1,000,000 (default $10). The opening stake is First bet % of the bank but never below $0.01; at a $1 bank, 1% is exactly $0.01, and anything smaller is raised to $0.01 (the price line says so).
                  • Deep check (gold panel or any open card): 5,000 sessions in the background with 95% intervals: Wilson intervals for ruin and finished-ahead, normal intervals for life, mean drop and mean result. The result is saved on the card and in memory (and exported). It can use the fast RNG or Stake's provably fair dice.
                  • Stress test: replays the card on the same 1,000 seeds under half the bank (same dollar opening stake), twice as many bets, a forced 12-loss streak at a random point, and a 2% edge, and shows how life, ruin and drop change.
                  • Stake's dice / Replay on seeds: Stake makes each roll from HMAC-SHA256(server seed, "client seed:nonce:0"), turns the first 4 bytes into a number between 0 and 1, and the roll is that × 10001 / 100 (00.00–100.00). Keel computes this itself (no browser crypto needed), so a replay can be checked roll by roll on Stake's verifier. Only use a server seed that Stake has already revealed (after rotating); the hashed seed shown should match Stake's.
                  • Simplify: removes conditions one at a time and keeps a removal when the score on the same 200 fresh sessions does not drop by more than the noise. It runs on its own whenever a new record wins its holdout. The shorter card is only offered; it must pass the normal confirmation and holdout to become the record. Auto-Simplify (automatic, on each holdout winner; since v17.1 also on one Goals-board or side-list card every 25 generations, kept only if it is just as good on 120 fresh paired sessions with no more ruin, breach or drop) removes conditions that never fired in 300 sessions or made no difference within ±0.002 on 120 (a stop-loss that ever fired is never removed), and is dropped (the original kept) if the shorter card scores lower on the same 60 holdout sessions or has a higher ruin, over-max-drop or 95th-percentile drop; the pinned card then says “Simplified: removed N conditions … score unchanged (±x) on the same 60 holdout sessions”, Show removed lists them and Show original switches the steps and Copy for Stake back to the unsimplified card. Its purpose is a shorter card to paste, not a higher score.
                  • Workers: the search splits each generation's sessions over up to 4 background workers (one fewer than your cores) and books the results in a fixed order, so the result is the same as on one worker, only faster. If workers are blocked it falls back to one worker or the page itself.
                  • Stake-native cards (v9): every card uses only Stake autoplay settings (amount, games, win chance, over/under, Play and Net Gain conditions). There are no bet caps; if the next bet is bigger than the balance the session ends. Bets never go below the opening stake ($0.01 minimum). Max drop, Min bets before a stop, and the rank are Keel-only scoring settings, labelled Keel-only in Copy for Stake.
                  • Last + green $ (the default rank since v16.1; the Analyst, the record and the deep check use it unless you pick another rank): score = life + dollar-weighted green − ruin, where dollar-weighted green = total won in winning sessions ÷ (total won + total lost in losing sessions). A card that wins small often and loses big (a hit-and-run net-gain stop) can be "green" in most sessions under Last + green but scores low here. Same drop and early-stop penalties as every rank.
                  • Cushion (v17 card family): plays a lively win chance while the session is down less than a set amount, switches to a safe win chance (usually 98%) once it is down more than that (and, in some cards, once it is up by a set amount), and goes back when it recovers. It uses only Stake Net Gain conditions with Set / Reset win chance, so the bet never changes and every card pastes into Stake as is. It does not beat the house: at $10 / 1% it trades a little of the house price for more bets played and more sessions finishing green (about 1.26 on Last + green $ vs about 1.14 for the best single win chance).
                  • Last + green (rank, v7; the default until v16.1, still selectable): score = life + green − ruin, where life here is the share of the table actually played (a stop at bet 250 of 1,000 is 25% life; a stop at bet 1 is 0.1%). Under the Last longer + go green ($-weighted) focus (default, saved; since v16.1 it scores with Last + green $) the Analyst asks trade-off questions (net-gain stops at several sizes, stops on win streaks, higher win-chance bands with a net-gain stop, capped escalation, stop timing 250 / 400 / 600 / 1000 via “On first streak of T Games, stop autoplay”), only sends on cards that play at least 90% of the table, and the holdout compares its cards on the same combined objective. It reports the trade-off as measured; going green more often is luck plus stopping, never an edge.
                  • Analyst (v6): a rule-based strategy analyst that runs inside the file, offline (no network, no AI service). It works on its own background worker next to the search, at the chosen Pace (Light 25%, Normal 50%, Heavy 100% of that worker). Each experiment asks one question (which condition matters, does a loss-streak stop cut drop, does softer escalation cut ruin, is a win-chance band underexplored, what a stop at the minimum bet does to the house price, swapped actions, untried condition combinations), builds the cards by deliberate edits, plays them on the same rolls (paired), and writes a note with 95% ranges and a verdict: helps, hurts, or no real difference (when the range overlaps zero it says so: that is noise). Cards that clearly score better are sent, labelled from Analyst, into the normal confirmation and holdout; the notebook reports how they fared. Running conclusions pool the evidence across experiments. Explain this card (any open card) removes each condition in turn and reports which ones drive life, ruin, drop, % ahead and price. The notebook and conclusions are saved in memory and included in Export. The Analyst never claims a card beats the house.
